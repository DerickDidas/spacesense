System and method

How it works

  • Data: NASA SMAP and MSL telemetry with JPL-labeled anomalies (Hundman et al., KDD 2018). Ten development channels are shown here.
  • Model: an LSTM (2 layers, 80 units, dropout 0.3) reads the last 250 steps of telemetry and command columns and predicts the next telemetry value. One model per channel, trained on that channel's training file.
  • Detection: absolute prediction error, smoothed (EWMA span 105). The threshold is recomputed per trailing window of 2,100 steps (stride 70) as mean plus z standard deviations, z from 2.5 to 10. No pruning in this view. Alerts within 50 steps of each other are merged.
  • Scoring: event-level. An anomaly counts as caught if any alert overlaps it; an alert that overlaps no labeled anomaly is a false alarm.

Known limitations

  • These are development channels whose labels were examined while building the method. They are not final benchmark numbers.
  • Many false alarms are short blips (1 to 10 steps) far from any labeled anomaly.
  • Event-level scoring is generous for long anomalies: one overlapping alert counts the whole event as caught.
  • Some anomalies are not detected at all (for example two of three on P-1).
  • This is a replay of public data. There is no connection to any spacecraft, and it is not a NASA project.
